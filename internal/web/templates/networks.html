Build your network from the bottom up — Uplink (one network port, or several teamed together) → vSwitch → Port Group (a VLAN) → VMs. Teaming can balance traffic automatically (no switch setup), use link aggregation, or act as active/standby failover. The management port is protected and can't be used as an uplink.

{{if .P.IsAdmin}}Tenant vSwitches — this organization's private networks{{else}}vSwitches{{end}}

{{if .P.OrgAdmin}}{{end}}

Each vSwitch is a private network that belongs to this organization alone — no other tenant can ever share it. Its untagged port group is where new VMs land; add VLAN port groups to split it into more networks. Connect a vSwitch to an uplink (provisioned by your provider) to carry it out to the physical network: an access uplink carries one port group (the one marked native), a QinQ or dedicated uplink carries all of them, each on its own VLAN. A port group carried out an uplink gets its gateway and DHCP from the upstream network. To route instead, leave a port group's gateway blank and attach it to a router on the vRouters page; give that router a leg on the uplink for internet access with NAT.
